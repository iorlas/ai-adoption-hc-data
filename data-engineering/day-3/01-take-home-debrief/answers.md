# Answers — 1 · Take-home debrief

**FACILITATOR ONLY. Never on screen.**

There is no answer key for this part. What there is instead: what to do with
what comes back, and the two ways this segment fails.

## The two failure modes

**Nobody did it.** Likely, and it is not a discipline problem — this cohort had
a four-day run and real jobs in between. Do not spend the fifteen minutes on
"why not." Pivot immediately: put **your own** before/after on screen (the Day-2
payoff prompt), spend five minutes, and give the other ten to part 2, which is
the part most likely to overrun.

**One person did it thoroughly and takes twelve minutes.** Just as likely. Cap
each person at two minutes out loud. The material you want in the room is
*breadth of stumbles*, not depth of one story.

## What to write on the board

Two columns: **stumbled on** / **wrote down**. By the end you want four or five
rows. That board is the argument for parts 2, 4 and 5 — all three end in a `docs/`
entry, and you can point back at it rather than re-justify.

## What to listen for, and follow up

- **"It invented a column."** The single most common one. Follow-up: *did the
  data dictionary exist at that point?* Almost always no.
- **"It kept suggesting the thing we already rejected."** That is the ADR case,
  exactly as Day 2 framed it. Ask whether the ADR got written.
- **"It was fine, no problems."** Usually means the task was small enough that
  context did not matter. Ask what they would have to add for it to work on the
  *whole* repo — that is the real answer, and it is the same answer as parts 2
  and 5.
- **"We can't use it on our real repo because of the data."** Do not brush past
  this. Restate the boundary: Claude read the code and the schema, not the rows.
  It is Day-2 governance and it will come back in Day 4.

## The knowledge-base review — what to say, carefully

These are their artifacts, in front of their colleagues, with their team lead in
the room. **Critique the file, never the person, and find something real to
credit in every one.**

The failure mode to name gently, because it will be present: a `CLAUDE.md` full
of instructions about tone and none about the domain. The line that lands
without stinging is *"this tells Claude how to behave; what would it need to
know to be useful here?"*

## Time

**Ten minutes, and hold it.** Part 2 needs its 40 and part 5 needs its 55 —
those two are what the client asked for in June, and this day has no spare
quarter-hour.
