# Before Tuesday — one check, on something real

Not homework. Nothing to hand in, nothing graded. A real ask, and we open
Session 4 by asking how it went — including *"I did not get to it"*, which is a
completely acceptable answer and will be treated as one.

Twenty minutes, if it goes well.

## The task

Pick **one** report you actually own. Then run **one** of today's two checks
against it.

### Option A — the definition check

> Open one measure from a real report. Ask Claude what it actually computes,
> then compare that against what the report *says* it shows and against what the
> person who asked for it thinks they are getting.

Copy the DAX out, paste it into Claude, and ask:

> Here is a measure from one of my reports. Tell me exactly what it counts and
> what it excludes, in plain English. Then tell me what someone reading a report
> labelled "[the label on the visual]" would reasonably assume it counts.

The gap between those two answers is the finding.

### Option B — the cross-check

> Find another report that carries a measure with the same or a similar name.
> Do they agree?

Paste both measures in:

> These two measures appear in different reports under similar names. Do they
> compute the same thing? Where they differ, what question is each one actually
> answering?

## If you would rather not use real work

Use the shared dataset. It still teaches the loop, and the two reports in
`reports/` are built to disagree.

If you would like this to land on campaign tagging instead, this is the moment
for the DotDigital tags and the Google Worksheet export.

## What to bring back

Three sentences is plenty:

1. What you checked.
2. Whether anything disagreed.
3. What you had to tell Claude that we did not have to tell it today.

**Do not send anyone your data.** Bring the finding, not the file. The third
question is the one that matters most for Tuesday, because on your own work
**the missing context is the work** — and that is exactly what Session 4 opens
on.

## What to do if it goes badly

Bring that instead. A confident wrong answer, an unusable one, or a place where
Claude simply could not know something — those are more useful in the room than
a clean success, and they are what Tuesday's opening is for.
