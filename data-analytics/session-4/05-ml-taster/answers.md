## What you do

**Say twice that nobody needs access** — start and end. Same reason as Monday's
Genie segment: the prerequisites promised no Databricks and someone will
otherwise assume they missed a setup step.

**One example, not five.** The example is Lucie's: which supporters are most
likely to give again in the next six months.

**Spend the most time on the part demos skip** — assembling the table. One row
per supporter, one column per thing you know, one column that is the answer you
are trying to predict. Getting there from five CSVs *is* the work, and it is the
honest thing to show.

**Do the leakage demonstration if time allows.** Put in a column that quietly
contains the answer, watch the model look brilliant, then name what happened. It
is the most memorable thirty seconds of the half hour and it is the mistake
everyone makes once.

**Then the eight minutes that justify the segment existing** — how you would
know whether to believe it. The four points are in `README.md`; do not cut them.

**Do not** call it magic, and **do not** suggest it replaces a judgement the
team currently makes.

## How to assert

Watch-only, so no gate. The check is one question to the room:

> **"If I showed you this model's output next month, what would you ask me
> before acting on it?"**

If someone says "how accurate is it", push once: *accurate how?* The right
answers are about the two error types, or about what data it was tested on. That
exchange is the segment's real outcome.

The other signal worth noticing: **does anyone ask for the dedicated session?**
If they do, that is a stronger result than any comprehension check, and it goes
straight into the close.

## What goes wrong

**It over-runs.** Most likely segment to. Rehearse it and time it. Cut the
leakage demo first, then the table-building narration.

**Someone asks for it on their data.** Do not improvise scope. Park it into the
close's named backlog: Databricks and ML properly, hands-on, for whoever wants
it.

**The workspace is slow.** Have the notebook and its output ready as a recorded
walkthrough. The teaching content here is the interpretation, not the run.

**It reads as a sales pitch.** The antidote is spending the eight minutes on how
to disbelieve it. If you are running short and tempted to cut that instead of
the demo, you have the priorities backwards.
